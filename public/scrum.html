The job in one sentence
A Scrum Master is the team's coach, not its boss.
They keep a small team working in short, focused bursts and clear away anything that slows it down. Think pit crew chief: they don't drive the car, but the car doesn't win without them.
What they do
  • Run a 15-minute team check-in every day
  • Clear roadblocks fast
  • Shield the team from distractions
  • Coach everyone on how Scrum works
What they don't do
  • Boss people around or hand out orders
  • Build the product themselves
  • Decide what gets built (the Product Owner does that)
Agile, in plain English
Build a little. Show it. Adjust.
Agile is a way of working in small steps with constant feedback. Scrum is the most common recipe for doing Agile, and the Scrum Master is the person who keeps that recipe running.

The Scrum cycle, usually 2 weeks
One sprint, start to finish
Step
What happens
The Scrum Master's part
1. Sprint Planning (day 1)
The team picks what it can finish in 2 weeks from the to-do list, called the backlog.
Runs the meeting and keeps the plan realistic.
2. Daily Scrum (15 min, every day)
Quick check-in: what I did, what's next, what's blocking me.
Keeps it short, then chases down blockers.
3. The work (days 2 to 9)
The team builds, tests and finishes pieces of the product.
Shields the team from interruptions and side requests.
4. Sprint Review (last day)
The team shows finished work to the people it's for and gets feedback.
Gets the right people in the room.
5. Retrospective (last day)
The team asks what worked, what didn't, and what to change next time.
Leads it and keeps it honest and blame-free.
Then it repeats, every 2 weeks.

Scrum runs on the scientific method: make a small bet, test it, check the results, adjust. The Scrum Guide calls it empiricism. Sound familiar?
Also common in banks, hospitals, insurance companies, government agencies and nonprofits.

Real talk
Before you jump in
1
A certificate opens the door. Experience gets you hired.
Entry-level roles got more competitive as more people certify. Employers want proof you've coached a team, even a small or volunteer one.
2
Your first title might not say Scrum Master.
Project Coordinator or junior Agile roles are a normal first step. Most Scrum Masters switched in from another job.
3
AI is changing the job, not ending it.
Software now handles tracking and reports, so the human side matters more: coaching, conflict and trust. That's your lane.
