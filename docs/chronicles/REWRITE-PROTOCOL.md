# Rewrite Protocol

After completing the audit, follow these steps in order.
Do not skip steps. Do not jump to rewriting before completing the full audit.

---

## Step 1: Identify the Core Idea

Before touching a single word, answer these three questions internally:

1. What is the single most important thing this piece is trying to say?
2. What is the most specific, interesting, or unexpected detail in the piece?
3. What is the emotional register? (Analytical, direct, warm, provocative, reflective?)

The rewrite must preserve all three. If the original doesn't have a specific interesting
detail, add one from context (Angelo's actual work, a named client, a real number).
Never invent facts. Ask for them if they're missing.

---

## Step 2: Fix the Opening First

The opening line or sentence is the highest-leverage fix. AI writing almost always
buries the most interesting thing. Humans lead with it.

Rules for the opening:
- The first sentence must be the most specific or unexpected thing in the piece
- It cannot be a setup sentence, a context-setter, or a thesis preview
- It should create a small amount of tension, curiosity, or surprise
- It must be under 20 words OR it must be complex enough to earn its length

Test: could this opening sentence stand alone as a social post? If yes, it's probably strong.

---

## Step 3: Rebuild Sentence Rhythm

Take the flagged sentences from Category 2 (structural flags) and restructure them.

Rhythm rules:
- After any sentence over 25 words, the next sentence should be under 12 words.
- Never have more than 3 sentences of similar length in a row.
- Short sentences (under 8 words) are powerful. Use them intentionally, not randomly.
- Long sentences earn their length through complexity, not padding.

Practical technique: read the rewritten paragraph aloud. If it sounds metronomic, it's wrong.
If it sounds like someone talking who knows what they're doing, it's right.

---

## Step 4: Replace Every Flagged Word

Work through the Lexical Flags (Category 1) systematically.

Replacement rules:
- Never do a direct synonym swap ("utilize" → "employ"). Rewrite the sentence.
- Every flagged transition word must be replaced by either:
  (a) cutting the transition entirely and letting the sentences connect naturally, or
  (b) starting the sentence in medias res, dropping straight into the idea
- Every hedging phrase must be replaced by a direct claim or cut entirely.
  If you can't make a direct claim, cut the sentence. Hedged sentences add no value.
- Vague quantifiers get replaced with specific numbers or named examples, or cut.

---

## Step 5: Break Structural Patterns

Work through the Structural Flags (Category 2).

- Parallel construction flag: change the verb, reorder the subject, or break the
  sentence into a different grammatical form. At least one of the three parallel
  sentences must be structurally different from the others.

- Setup-and-summarize flag: cut the preview paragraph entirely. Cut or compress
  the closing summary to one line that adds a new thought, not a recap.

- List-of-three reflex: reduce to two items, or expand to a full sentence
  that explains the relationship between the items instead of just listing them.

---

## Step 6: Inject a Specific Detail

If the audit flagged "no specific details" (Category 3, item 3.5), add at least one:

- A named client, project, or product
- A real number (percentage, time, user count, score)
- A specific design decision and its observable outcome
- A named research method and what it revealed
- A date or timeline

If the information is not available in context, add a placeholder in [brackets]
and note it in the Audit Report as "Needs specific detail: [describe what's missing]."

---

## Step 7: Fix the Ending

AI writing ends with a summary or a generic call to inspiration. Human writing ends
with a provocation, a specific question, an unanswered tension, or simply stops
when it has nothing more to say.

Rules for endings:
- No "In conclusion" or "Ultimately" openers
- No restatement of points just made
- No generic forward-looking statements ("As designers, we must...")
- End options: a pointed question, a specific unresolved tension, a one-line
  observation that reframes everything above, or simply the last substantive point

For LinkedIn posts specifically: end with a question that has a real answer worth
discussing, not a performative "What do you think?" The question should be specific
enough that a wrong answer is possible.

---

## Step 8: Apply Brand Rules (Mode A Only)

If running in Mode A (ChrowmDesigns content), load `brand-voice-rules.md` and run
a final pass against those rules after completing Steps 1-7.

Brand pass must verify:
- ChrowmDesigns named at least once (pieces over 150 words)
- No em dashes anywhere
- All results are results, not tasks
- No generic diplomacy phrases
- No theory-first framing

---

## Step 9: Post-Rewrite Self-Check

Before delivering the output, run this verification:

**Rhythm check**: Read the first 5 sentences aloud. Do they have varied length and tempo?
If they sound uniform, go back to Step 3.

**Opener check**: Is the first sentence the most interesting thing in the piece?
If not, go back to Step 2.

**Flag check**: Are there zero remaining flags from the original audit?
If any remain, fix them before delivering.

**Specificity check**: Is there at least one concrete, named, or numbered detail?
If not, go back to Step 6.

**Ending check**: Does the piece end with something new, not a summary?
If not, go back to Step 7.

All five must pass. If any fail, do not deliver. Fix and re-check.

---

## What "Clean" Means

Clean writing is not writing that sounds generic. It is writing that sounds like a
specific person with real experience and a real point of view. The target register
for all ChrowmDesigns content is: a senior designer talking to a peer at a good agency.
Direct. Specific. A little self-aware. Zero performance.

The goal is not to sound less like AI. It is to sound more like Angelo.
