## Combined direction

Surreal editorial compositions that pair a physical gesture with precise graphic design. One focal action per image. A hand aligning a displaced module expresses diagnosis before redesign without literal software imagery.

- **Composition:** One asymmetrical focal group, generous negative space, a clear reading order. Landscape and square need their own compositions.
- **Color:** Warm blush/ivory foundation; teal and lilac as principal accents; pale lime in a small module; coral as a light source or focal accent; black or deep navy for contrast. Do not give every color equal area.
- **Material:** Pale stone with fine grain, smooth satin geometry, and a restrained reflective dark surface. Light should connect the objects.
- **Typography:** Large black neo-grotesk, tight but readable spacing, short copy, thin editorial rules. Keep title text legible on a phone. No imitation of reference brands.
- **Human element:** A single purposeful sculptural hand; plausible anatomy even when its setting is surreal.
- **Meaning:** A visible displacement and an intentional adjustment. No claims of measured results.
- **Avoid:** Generic stock-tech scenes, tiny inspecting people, magnifying glasses, literal browser chrome, cluttered motif collages, decorative charts, and the previous beige paper-craft direction.

## Production method

The image tool accepts at most five input reference files. First pass uses references 1–5 and the written synthesis. Second pass supplies that generated composition and reference 6 to apply its editorial hierarchy while preserving the first five contributions. The square adaptation follows the same complete foundation.

## Acceptance checks

Each final image must show evidence of all six contributions: (1) portal/gesture and color-plane logic; (2) sculptural hand with simple geometry; (3) reflective depth and coral light; (4) strong typographic scale and editorial rule; (5) modular colored components with poster clarity; (6) warm ground, controlled spacing and adaptation to the format.

No requirement to reproduce every literal object from every reference. The foundation combines their design decisions into one new concept.

### Shared identity, varied images

Read and consider all twelve references before choosing an image direction. They form a collection-level vocabulary, not twelve objects that every image must contain. Shared qualities are editorial clarity, surreal intervention, deliberate color, texture and controlled negative space. Every cover gets its own subject, metaphor, medium, palette, crop and camera. No compulsory stone hand, sphere, arch, mirror floor or beige ground. Photographic terrain, portrait photography, flat collage, textile macro, graphic abstraction and occasional sculpture are all valid.

The no-pink/red instruction applies to the Chronicles website UI. The newly supplied references permit expressive artwork colors; use orange/violet/cobalt/lime alternatives where appropriate rather than importing the reference blog's pink interface. Do not make every cover teal and ivory.

Across any five neighboring covers, use at least four distinct media/composition families. Reject a new cover if it resembles another after shrinking to thumbnail size, even if its central object changed. No repeated architectural still-life set. Compare the actual generated results, not only the prompts. Repeat this review after all twenty exist.

Use real SVG branding in the website presentation layer; do not have the model invent the mark. Keep raster artwork clean, with an explicit logo overlay in the article figure. Store the complete prompt and unique concept in the editorial ledger.
