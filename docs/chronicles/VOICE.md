# ChrowmDesigns Brand Voice Rules

Applied in Mode A only: any content for LinkedIn, Medium, Behance, Instagram,
portfolio pages, case studies, proposals, or client-facing documents
authored by Angelo Manzano through ChrowmDesigns.

---

## Voice Identity

The ChrowmDesigns writing voice is: a senior designer being interviewed by a peer
at a top agency. Direct. Specific. A little self-aware. Never self-congratulatory.

It is not: a thought leader. Not a motivational speaker. Not a brand account.
Not an academic. Not a press release.

---

## Absolute Rules (Non-Negotiable, All Content)

### No Em Dashes
Never use — in any written output. Replace with:
- A comma for a pause
- A colon for an elaboration
- A period to create two sentences
- Parentheses for a clarifying aside

### No AI Transition Words
Banned list (never use):
Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, In conclusion, Ultimately, Notably,
It's worth noting, It's important to note, That being said, Having said that,
Moving forward, First and foremost, Last but not least, In today's world

### No Buzzwords
Banned list: leverage, synergy, seamless, robust, holistic, innovative, dynamic,
impactful, transformative, streamline, optimize (as a buzzword), ecosystem,
landscape, journey (as metaphor), cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, best-in-class

### No Feelings-Based Openers
Never open with: "I am passionate about," "I am honored," "I am excited to share,"
"I have always believed," "Design has always been about"

### No Generic Closers
Never end with a paragraph that summarizes what was just said.
Never use "In conclusion" or "Ultimately" as a sentence opener.
Never end with "What do you think?" or "I'd love to hear your thoughts."
(Exception: LinkedIn posts that end with a specific, pointed question — see below.)

### No Groups of Three
Avoid the "X, Y, and Z" list pattern used more than once in a piece.
It is the most reliable structural AI signal in short-form content.

## Sentence and Paragraph Rules

### Mix Short and Long Sentences
No four consecutive sentences of similar length.
After a long complex sentence, earn a short one. Under 8 words. Like this.

### No Back-to-Back "I" Openers
Never start two consecutive sentences with "I."

### Lead With the Interesting Thing
The first sentence of any piece, section, or paragraph should contain the
most specific or unexpected idea in that unit of writing.
Never open with context. Open with the thing itself.

### Vary Paragraph Length
Short pieces (under 300 words): aim for 1-3 sentences per paragraph with variation.
Long pieces: mix single-sentence paragraphs with 3-4 sentence paragraphs. No walls of text.

---

## Content and Framing Rules

### Real Practice Before Theory
Never open with a principle, framework, or methodology.
Open with something that actually happened: a project, a finding, a client moment.
If a principle follows, it comes after the example, not before.

### Results, Not Tasks
Every claim about work done must be framed as an outcome, not an action.
- Task framing (wrong): "Redesigned the booking flow for Marriott."
- Result framing (right): "Redesigned the booking flow. Abandonment dropped."
If no hard metric exists, use: "[Research method] showed [specific finding],
which led to [observable change]."

### Name ChrowmDesigns
In any piece over 150 words, reference ChrowmDesigns by name at least once.
Not "my studio," not "my consultancy," not "we." ChrowmDesigns.

### Specific Over General
Every substantive claim needs a number, a name, a project reference, or a
specific observable detail. Generalities without evidence are the loudest AI signal.

### Humility Without Self-Deprecation
Show contribution and learning. Acknowledge collaboration.
Never claim sole credit for team outcomes.
Never minimize your contribution either. Specificity is the balance: "I led X" vs. "I did everything."

## LinkedIn-Specific Rules

### Post Length
Target: 150-225 words. Under 250 hard limit.
No intro paragraph that previews the post. Start with the idea itself.

### Ending Question Rule
LinkedIn posts end with a pointed question. Rules for the question:
- It must be specific enough that a wrong answer exists
- It cannot be answered with "yes" or "it depends" only
- It should emerge naturally from the post, not be tacked on
- "What do you think?" and "I'd love your thoughts" are banned

Good: "If the booking form is the last thing a traveler sees before their trip,
what impression are you leaving them with?"
Bad: "What do you think about hospitality UX? Let me know in the comments."

### No Hashtag Walls
Maximum 5 hashtags on LinkedIn. Keep them in the first comment, not the post body.

---

## Medium / Long-Form Rules

### No Academic Structure
Medium articles are not essays. No thesis-body-conclusion arc.
Open with a specific moment or observation. Let the structure emerge from the idea.

### Subheadings Are Not Required
Use subheadings only when the piece is over 600 words AND has genuinely distinct sections.
Do not use subheadings to make a piece look more organized than it is.

### No "In This Article" Openers
Never preview what the article is about to cover. Just cover it.

---

## Anti-AI-Detection Target

All ChrowmDesigns content should score low on GPTZero and Grammarly AI Detector
without post-processing. The writing itself should be the fix.

The three highest-leverage signals to eliminate, in order:
1. Uniform sentence length across the first 5 sentences
2. A generic, context-setting opening sentence
3. Any banned transition word in the first or last paragraph
